Answer

Conclusion
  1. 1Gap. The grammar and its hook check a row’s shape and stop at the bold title, so nothing reads the words after it — and those words are what the owner sees on the card.
  2. 2Rule. After the title, at most three short sentences: what the work is, why it exists, what happens next. Evidence sits in parentheses at the end of the clause it supports.
  3. 3Home. The rule joins the “Filing” section of ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md, beside the item grammar, and its length half becomes a pool contract key the existing hook compiles.
  4. 4State. Nothing was decided here when this was written. Accepting costs one commit; the hook change and the sweep of old rows follow as their own ticket. The owner accepted on 2026-08-12 — see the decision note above.

Problem

Conclusion
  1. 1Shape only. The pool contract declares the bullet, the date, the tag run and a bold title lead, and check-backlog-format.py compiles exactly those, so the text after the title is never read (~/.claude a6938f0).
  2. 2That text is the card. The board cuts the bold title at 120 characters for the card face and prints the whole row as the card body (board/src/board/server/parser.py:147-194, board 204f3b6).
  3. 3Asked twice. The owner asked for readable rows on 2026-08-08 and again on 2026-08-12. The second request was itself filed as an unreadable row — 275 characters in one sentence.
  1. marker — field, checked. [ ] open or [/] working; the board groups cards by it.
  2. date — field, checked. The day the item was created, never the day it moved.
  3. tag run — field, checked. The declared #tag kinds, each able to carry its own reason in parentheses. The board lifts the run off the card and draws it as chips.
  4. title — field, checked for shape only. The hook requires a bold run and reads nothing inside it; the board cuts it at 120 characters and prints it as the card face.
  5. body — field, unchecked. Everything after the title, plus any indented bullet under the row. The board prints it whole as the card body. This field is the whole subject of this proposal.

Options

Conclusion
  1. 1Three axes. Readability — does the card get easier to read; enforcement — can a second reader fail a bad row; reach — does the session writing the row load the rule at the moment it writes.
  2. 2Two choices, judged apart. The structuring rule is judged on readability and enforcement; its home is judged on reach and enforcement.

The rule

OptionWhat it doesCostWhat it gives up
Three-part body After the title, at most three short sentences in a fixed order: what the work is, why it exists, what happens next. Evidence sits in parentheses at the end of the clause it supports. No new mechanism. Every writer must hold the order and the plain words in mind. Full enforcement. Only the length is machine-checkable; the order and the plainness stay author discipline.
Detail in bullets The top line keeps the title and one sentence. Why, next steps and evidence move to indented bullets under the row. More file lines per row, and facts split from the sentence they qualify. The gain itself. The parser folds an indented line into the same card body, so the card reads exactly as before (board 204f3b6). Split facts also drift apart on later edits.
Length caps only No structure rule. The hook caps the title and the body; anything longer links out to a document. One contract key and one hook branch. Readability. A cap rewards recompression, and dense shorthand is the shortest way under any cap — the exact style the owner rejected.

The home

OptionWhat it doesCostWhat it gives up
Beside the grammar The prose rule joins “Filing” in ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md, next to the item grammar. The checkable half becomes a pool contract key — a character budget for the text after the title — which check-backlog-format.py compiles like the rest. A contract change, a hook change with tests, and one sweep of rows over budget. Room for worked examples. “Filing” is already long, so the examples stay in a document like this one rather than beside the rule.
In the feedback memory Leaves the rule where it is, as feedback-backlog-wording in the workspace pool. Nothing. Reach. A memory is pool-scoped, so a session writing another pool’s backlog never loads it — which is how the 2026-08-12 row was written four days after the 2026-08-08 feedback.
In a workspace spec A spec under ~/workspace/docs/ holds the rule, and “Filing” points at it. One new document plus the pointer. Reach again, and one home. Nothing loads a docs spec while a row is being written, and the rule would sit apart from the grammar it extends.

Recommendation

Conclusion
  1. 1Three-part body, beside the grammar. The structure carries the readability and the budget catches the overflow. Detail in bullets is refuted by the parser, which folds it back into the same card.
  2. 2Reach decided the home. A memory reaches only its own pool, and the status quo has already failed once — four days after the feedback was filed.
  3. 3The flip. If rows keep coming back dense with the rule in “Filing”, then reach was not the binding constraint, and the next move is a check on the words, not another home.
  • The first step is one commit. Add the writing rule to “Filing” and declare the body budget in the pool contract block, together. The hook, the memory and the sweep follow as their own ticket.
  • The memory retires into a pointer. Filing’s own promotion rule moves a takeaway into ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md once a second, independent task confirms it, and leaves the evidence behind. The 2026-08-12 request is that second confirmation.
  • The budget is a floor, not the rule. About 360 characters approximates three short sentences. Neither row below exceeds it, so the budget alone would have passed both — it catches overflow, and the prose rule carries the rest.

Omissions

Four things this proposal does not claim — and the Commit field pins ~/workspace alone
  • The pin’s reach. ~/workspace holds none of the evidence. The grammar and the hook sit in ~/.claude at a6938f0, the parser in board at 204f3b6, and the pools are not versioned at all.
  • The 360-character number. It is an estimate of three short sentences, not a measurement. The real number is the owner’s to set when the option is accepted.
  • How many rows break the rule. No sweep was run, so the size of step 5 is unknown.
  • Whether the rule works. Only two owner requests support it. Nothing here shows that a written rule changes what a session writes.
