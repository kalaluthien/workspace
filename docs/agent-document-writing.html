Two template ancestries diverged, and the drafted change already removed one of them

camera/docs/templates/ holds nine files: five view templates (explanation, benchmark, how-to-guide, mockup, proposal) and four spec templates. camera/docs/README.md is 13 KB of rules that directory owns alone — its chapter table, its Category field, its script/check-docs.py commit hook. workspace/docs/templates/ carried three view templates on a separate line of descent: principle, pattern, practice, added at aac9e27 and deleted at 3e3d22f. board/docs/ has no templates/ directory at all; board/docs/INDEX.md and board/.claude/CLAUDE.md both send an author to those same three workspace paths, which the pinned commit removed. Board therefore writes today against a template set that no longer exists.

Twelve of the fifteen views already written across the three repositories still carry the retired Category field, and spend six distinct genre names between them — principle 4, explanation 3, pattern 2, practice 1, benchmark 1, proposal 1. A reader cannot tell from the genre alone what kind of claim a document makes until they know which repository it came from. The drift is still growing: board/docs/ has gained a seventh view, service-surfaces.html, written as a principle — a genre whose template the parent deleted at the pinned commit.

No template on either ancestry carried a rubric. Every one of camera’s nine and all three deleted workspace templates contain the word zero times; each of the four current templates carries one rubric block. What existed instead was required-sections and required-elements, and camera/docs/README.md §Enforcement states outright that both are “not checked anywhere”. Review quality was therefore whatever the reviewer remembered on the day — a cost paid by the owner, who is the reviewer, on every delivered document.

The choice is how many doctypes the parent defines, and where the shared skeleton lives

Six axes, named before any option is judged. Consistency: does one reader’s question get one name in every repository. Cost per new document: what an author pays to start one, including choosing the doctype. Cost per rule change: how many files one change to a shared rule touches. Reviewability: can a reviewer score a document without prior knowledge of the system. Self-containment: does the delivered file open from file:// with no build step and no external reference. Migration cost: what the twelve views still on the old field and the two remaining catalogues owe.

OptionWhat it doesCostWhat it gives up
Separate ancestries Restores the three deleted workspace templates and leaves camera’s nine and board’s borrowing as they were. A revert. Nothing else, ever. Consistency and reviewability, permanently. Board keeps depending on another repository’s files with nothing enforcing they stay.
Rubrics only Keeps every repository’s template set and its genre names; adds a rubric block to each of the twelve pre-existing templates. Twelve edits, no migration, no catalogue change. A rubric must be written twice wherever two names cover one question. Consistency. A document still means different things in different directories, and a rule change is still paid once per ancestry.
Maximal split One doctype per genre, as drafted at 3e3d22f: five view templates (adding report and mockup) and five spec templates. Ten templates to keep consistent with each other; ten rubrics that drift apart; an author choosing between five view doctypes before writing a line. Minimality. A shape that recurs once demands a new template rather than a section, and the choice cost lands on every document forever.
Minimized parent system Three view doctypes (explanation, guide, proposal) and one spec.md whose section shapes cover decisions, rules, procedures, capabilities and ledgers; a rubric in each; components/ as the doctype-independent layer; the document-writer agent as the writer. Twelve views carry the retired Category field; camera’s nine templates and its check-docs.py hook still assert the old vocabulary; board’s catalogue needs re-pointing. A base-style change is edited in eight files. Per-repository genre names that fit one repository only, and single-sourcing: every view template holds a full copy of the base style by rule.
Generated skeletons Makes components/ the only hand-edited source and generates each template from it with a build step; templates become derived artifacts. A generator, a runner for it in each repository, and either generated files committed or a staleness check that fails when they drift. “An author copies a template once and then owns the result” (2026-07-28, owner decision, camera/docs/README.md). The template stops being readable as the whole thing it produces.

Two overlaps are deliberate and worth naming. Rubrics only is a proper subset of both parent-system options on the reviewability axis; it is listed separately because it tests whether the rubric gap alone justified the vocabulary change, or whether those are two wants that arrived together. Maximal split and Minimized parent system are the same system at two granularities and differ on one axis only, doctype count — which is why the count, not the system, is what was actually at stake. Generated skeletons is the only option that attacks the cost-per-rule-change axis, which is why it stays in the document despite contradicting a recorded decision: an option removed for its conclusion cannot be re-proposed with its reason attached.

Three doctypes and one spec template close naming and review at the lowest standing cost

Minimized parent system wins because it is the only option positive on consistency, reviewability and self-containment at once. Separate ancestries and Rubrics only cannot reach consistency by construction — they preserve the ancestries that caused the drift. Maximal split reaches consistency but pays for it on cost per new document and cost per rule change, and it loses on the axis the owner named: the system is “deliberately minimal (2026-08-02, owner decision) … a shape that recurs becomes a section pattern inside an existing template, never a new doctype” (docs/README.md). The folds that decision forced are defensible on their own terms — a dated finding is an explanation whose ground is a re-runnable protocol, and a mockup is a proposal whose option is drawn — so the collapse costs no distinction a reader was relying on.

Generated skeletons still beats the recommendation on exactly one axis, cost per rule change: one edit against eight. That axis is real and should not be waved away — of the five commits that have ever touched docs/templates/, one (9b9428a, a white background) was a pure shared-style change, and it was paid as five edits across three templates and both existing views. The same change now costs eight edits, three view templates and five components, and still does not reach a document already written, which owns its copy by rule. What outweighs it is that the build step has to land in every repository and argue against a self-containment rule the system states twice, while the axes it loses on have no such workaround. Minimizing the doctype count already bought back a fifth of that cost: the same change would have been ten edits under Maximal split.

That makes the flip condition near rather than hypothetical: the next shared-style change will cost eight edits against the five that 9b9428a cost, and if a third follows it, Generated skeletons buys back more than its indirection costs and the 2026-07-28 copy-once decision should be reopened rather than worked around. It also flips if camera’s check-docs.py hook turns out to cost more to teach the Doctype field than the whole migration saves — that is one read of one file and has not been done.

The smallest first step is this branch, and no more than this branch: adopt the system for workspace/docs alone, which its uncommitted state already carries — four templates in place, docs/INDEX.md re-chaptered as Specifications, Explanations, Guides and Proposals, and the two existing views restated from Category to Doctype as explanation and guide. What remains before the step is complete is one camera document written against the new templates as a live test of them. The camera-wide migration — its nine templates, its catalogue and its hook — is a separate change; the owner deferred backward compatibility for it (2026-08-02, owner decision). One commit accepts this; nothing in it obliges the second.

Undecided Board is still accruing documents in the retired vocabulary — seven views on Category, the newest written as a principle after the parent deleted that template. Whether board migrates its catalogue as part of this change or keeps writing against the old three names until a later one is not recorded anywhere. The mapping itself is settled: a principle whose decision is open becomes a proposal, one whose decision is made becomes a decision section in spec.md and leaves the view set entirely — so board’s count of views is not conserved by the migration.
